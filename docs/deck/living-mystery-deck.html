WHY IT'S HARD

Five things a good generated mystery must get right

It is easy to generate a case that is technically solvable but trivial: every clue in view at once, clues that name the culprit, nothing that misleads. A real mystery has to hold all five of these at the same time.

THE CORE IDEA

The model proposes; the engine decides

Language models have unbounded imagination and no guarantees. A small deterministic core has guarantees and no imagination. Each does only what it is good at, and meaning always lives in typed data, never in prose.

ARCHITECTURE

The system at a glance

A zero-build browser game over a DOM-free engine. Small local servers carry model calls, solver checks and case generation; every one of them writes debug logs to logs/.

THE FULL PICTURE

One true history; a model proposes at every layer, critics decide

From a premise to an accusation, every layer has the same shape: a model does the creative work, then code, a second model and the certifying kernels check it before it counts. The generated history is the only source of facts.

WHAT IS NEW

Eight things this system does that a story generator does not

01
Certified, not just generated

Every case is proved fair and solvable by two independent kernels and Z3: exactly one suspect fits, every lie can be broken, motive, means and opportunity can be found.

02
Depth by construction

Three operators peel a mystery layer by layer; code schedules them toward a target depth, and a computed difficulty labels the result.

03
One source of facts

The generated history is the only truth. Every account is backed by something its speaker did, saw or knew; nothing added later invents a fact.

04
Free speech, bounded knowledge

Type anything to anyone. Characters answer in their own voice from what they know; a fact-checker that knows the clock and their stories keeps them honest, or lying only where the story says.

05
Witnesses that cannot spoil it

Who saw whom is read from the history and kept only where the solve is unchanged, so a witness can never shortcut the puzzle.

06
A village that reacts

A model plans the morning over the whole story; people seek each other out, pass on news and hide evidence, each step grounded in what they know and checked first.

07
Autonomy that stays solvable

A culprit may destroy a clue only if the kernel proves the case still certifies without it: drama without ever trapping the player.

08
Measured end to end

Every case is played to a solution by code; every character is asked a standard question set with the real model; every account is proved reachable from the menu.

THE GAME ENGINE

Every change is an event with a cause

The engine is event-sourced. Actions become causal events with reversible patches; the world can be rebuilt at any moment, and characters know only what they perceived or were told.

LIVING CHARACTERS

Characters speak freely, but only from what they know

For a free-text question, the character receives only its own perspective. The model picks one of the accounts the engine allows (true, or a permitted lie) and phrases it in voice. Code and an independent critic reject invented facts.

LIVES AND ACTIONS

Characters also act, and their actions are checked too

Between conversations the village keeps living. Ordinary routines are authored; optional model deliberation chooses among bounded plans. Any plan that could touch evidence must pass the same playability check as the player's turn.

DAILY LIFE
Routines and appointments

Each character has a home, duties and meetings. The clock drives travel and arrivals; witnesses see only what happens where they are.

AUTONOMY
Bounded plans

A model may pick a plan from a typed catalogue (move, take, give, conceal, report, correct). It runs detached, never blocking the player, and is dropped if the world changed.

ADMISSION
No plan may break the case

Plans are shape-checked, optionally proved in Z3, and only committed if a validated route to the solution still exists afterwards.

A LIVING VILLAGE

The morning moves, and can never trap the player

Every fifteen game minutes, or as soon as the detective does something that matters (shows a clue, finds one about someone, catches a lie), one model call plans the next half hour for the people it affects. Three critics check the plan before anything happens; the engine runs what survives on the game clock.

NO DEAD ENDS

How the solver keeps every case playable

Every turn — the player's, a character's, the Director's — first runs on a copy of the world. It is kept only if the case can still be solved from the resulting state. Otherwise it is discarded as if it never happened.

NARRATIVE DESIGN · THE KERNEL

Four primitives and a question describe any mystery

No murder, manor or fixed cast is built in. Settings supply their own nouns and domain rules; the kernel supplies the logic of evidence.

BEING
Entity

Anything with identity: people, places, objects, documents, groups — with traits.

BECOMING
Event

Happens over a time span at a place, with participants, preconditions, effects — and the attitude that caused it.

MIND
Attitude

A stance toward a proposition: believes, knows, wants, fears, intends, feels. Motive, secrets, lies.

LAW
Rule

How things generally go — strict or a default with exceptions. Commonsense and each setting's domain rules.

FRAME
Question

What the player must determine, with a finite set of candidate answers. Solved when exactly one survives.

Signs are not primitives. A clue is any observable effect of an event: a trace on an object, a document, a speech (testimony), a behaviour. Reading a sign means reasoning back to its cause. A lie is a speech whose speaker believes otherwise; a staged scene is a physical lie. Minds act on the world; the world changes minds.

LOGIC ONLY WHERE IT ADDS VALUE

Clues carry typed facts; the engine works out what they prove

Models are unreliable at multi-step logic. So the model writes each clue's text plus one small typed fact, and fixed engine code decides which suspects that fact rules out, whether it is true or a lie, and how deep in the case it sits. The model never gets to assert “this clears Rowan”.

RECURSIVE REFINEMENT

Three operators peel a mystery layer by layer

A player learns a fact by accessing a sign, reading it and trusting it. Difficulty can only live in those three links — so there are exactly three operators, applied recursively.

STORY GENERATION

Code decides the premise and the depth; the model writes the story

Generation starts from a trivial, certified case and deepens it one justified beat at a time. Every step is checked by code and reviewed by a second model before it is kept.

HOW DEPTH IS MEASURED

One thread per suspect

A case is solved when every innocent suspect has been ruled out and the culprit is confirmed. Each of those is a thread, and its depth is how many discovery steps it takes to settle. The case is solved when its deepest thread is settled, so that sets the solve depth. The generator always deepens the thread that is settled soonest, so no suspect can be cleared on the first page and every suspect takes real work.

IN THE GAME

Generating a case, and getting unstuck

One button starts a generation on the local server. The player sees high-level progress; a collapsed spoiler shows everything the generator is writing, for testing. While playing, hints and debug logs keep the player and the developer moving.

PROGRESS
What is happening now

The current stage, elapsed time and live activity — “writing 2 drafts in parallel”, “1 passed the checks”, “a second model is reviewing them” — with solve depth, misdirection, red herring, motive and means filling in.

SPOILER
The story being built

Collapsed by default: the question and answer, the cast with their secrets, the hidden history, and every layer — its story beat, blocks, the records it added, and the drafts that were rejected.

HINTS
One nudge at a time

The next useful step, told three ways: a nudge, a clue, then the plain answer. Hints move on as the player does, and end by pointing to the accusation.

LOGS
Debuggable by design

logs/game.log records actions, every dialogue turn with its audit, and errors; the generator and proxy log too. Flag an issue saves a note with a snapshot of the game.

FAIR AND VARIED

Who, how and why — and a different story every time

Code decides the premise and proves the case fair three ways; the model writes the story and plays the people.

PREMISE DECK
No favourite story

Code draws the victim, culprit, suspects, method, motive, scene and shape, rotating away from recent cases. A first draft that does not honour the draw is rejected.

3
independent certifiers

a case is published only when the Python kernel, the JS kernel and Z3 all certify it and agree

Honest limits. The kernel certifies fair, solvable, deep and misleading — not fun. The fact-checker and the morning director are models’ judgements: about 96% of replies come from the model, and the director often lets people carry on quietly. A case takes 5–25 minutes to build (one is kept ready); touch screens have no drag and drop.

TAKEAWAYS
1
A mystery is four primitives and a question.

Entities, events, attitudes and rules describe any setting; clues are just effects of events.

2
Depth comes from justified refinement.

Conceal, Fragment, Mislead — each a story beat with a cause — applied to the shallowest thread.

3
Models imagine; code decides.

The model never states what a clue proves or what happens next; the engine computes it and certifies it.

4
Playability is a checked invariant.

Every turn is tried on a copy and kept only if the case can still be solved.
